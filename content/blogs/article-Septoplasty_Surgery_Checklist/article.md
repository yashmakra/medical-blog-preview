# Septoplasty Surgery Checklist: Pre-Op Evaluation & Nasal Surgery Prep

A bent nasal septum can make breathing through one side of the nose difficult, but it is not the only cause of blockage. Allergy-related swelling, enlarged turbinates, polyps or problems with the outer nasal framework may contribute. Before septoplasty, the ENT surgeon should explain which part of your obstruction the operation is expected to improve and what symptoms may need other treatment.

This septoplasty surgery checklist covers the assessment before surgery, questions about combined procedures, medicine and fasting instructions, and the arrangements needed while the nose heals. Septoplasty straightens the internal partition; it is different from rhinoplasty, which changes the shape of the nose, although they can sometimes be performed together.

For patients in India, the preparation also includes confirming whether a functional or cosmetic procedure is proposed, what the estimate includes and how postoperative review will be arranged. Bring details of earlier nasal injuries, operations, sprays and sleep-breathing problems. Ask for individual instructions rather than copying the recovery schedule of someone who had a different nasal operation.

## Make sure the diagnosis matches the operation

The septum is the cartilage-and-bone partition between the nasal passages. Septoplasty reshapes selected parts while preserving support. It does not remove every cause of congestion and is not a guarantee that snoring, allergy symptoms or sleep apnoea will resolve.

Describe whether blockage is constant or changes sides, whether it follows an injury and whether nasal medicines have helped. Tell the surgeon about nosebleeds, loss of smell, sinus symptoms and previous nasal surgery. Examination may include looking inside the nose with a small scope where appropriate.

If turbinate treatment, sinus surgery or rhinoplasty is also proposed, ask for each component to be explained separately. The [CUH explanation of septorhinoplasty](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/information-after-septorhinoplasty/) distinguishes correction of the septum from changing the outer nose. Its recovery details concern a combined operation and should not be applied automatically to isolated septoplasty.

## What may be assessed before admission?

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Nasal examination | Identifies septal deviation and other sources of blockage | At the ENT consultation |
| Nasal endoscopy | Examines areas not easily seen at the nostril | According to symptoms and findings |
| CT of the sinuses | Answers a sinus-disease or combined-surgery question | Not automatically needed for every septoplasty |
| Bleeding and medicine history | Identifies factors affecting bleeding management | Before surgery |
| Anaesthetic review and selected tests | Plans care around existing health conditions | As directed by the treating team |

Bring previous imaging if it is already available; do not obtain a scan simply because it appears in a surgery package. Mention bleeding after dental work, easy bruising and a family history of bleeding problems. These details can be more useful than requesting the same test panel for everyone.

## Medicines, anaesthesia and nasal care

List prescribed medicines, non-prescription painkillers, nasal sprays, supplements and any blood thinners. Ask which treatments should continue and which need a clinician-directed change. Do not stop an anticoagulant or begin antibiotics independently. Explain medicine allergies and previous reactions to local anaesthetic or general anaesthesia.

Septoplasty is often performed under general anaesthesia, although the final technique depends on the procedure and patient. Discuss sleep apnoea, CPAP use, breathing problems and previous nausea. If you use a mask over the nose, ask how it will be managed immediately after surgery rather than making your own adjustment.

Obtain written fasting instructions from the anaesthesia team, including the plan for diabetes medicines. Tell staff if you develop a cold, fever, chest symptoms or a new infection before admission. The team decides whether assessment or postponement is needed.

Ask whether any preoperative spray, rinse or skin preparation is prescribed. Avoid unrequested decongestant courses or home remedies. A nose that feels temporarily clearer after a spray does not by itself establish whether septal surgery is necessary.

## Prepare for temporary blockage after surgery

The nose may initially feel blocked because of swelling, crusting, dressings or splints. This does not immediately show whether the operation has achieved its longer-term aim. Ask whether packing or splints are expected and how removal or review is arranged.

The [Gloucestershire NHS septoplasty aftercare leaflet](https://www.gloshospitals.nhs.uk/your-visit/patient-information-leaflets/septoplasty-aftercare-advice-ent/) provides procedure-specific aftercare information. Your surgeon should specify when to rinse, use sprays, blow the nose or resume exercise. Do not remove an internal dressing or splint yourself.

Arrange transport after anaesthesia and help at home if needed. Discuss time away from dusty work, strenuous exercise or situations where the nose could be knocked. Choose comfortable clothing and plan meals and drinks that are manageable while mouth breathing. Ask for written instructions you can refer to after discharge.

## Confirm what the estimate covers

Ask the hospital to separate functional septoplasty from any proposed cosmetic work in the clinical plan and estimate. Clarify anaesthesia, dressings, splint removal and follow-up charges. Insurance policies may treat components differently, so authorisation should match the operation agreed during consent. If you are travelling for surgery, allow for the review needed before leaving the area and ask where an unexpected nosebleed can be assessed. Financial approval does not establish that every proposed component is medically necessary.

## Septoplasty preparation checklist

### Before the operation date

- [ ] Understand what septoplasty is expected to improve.
- [ ] Clarify whether turbinate, sinus or cosmetic procedures are included.
- [ ] Share previous nasal operation and injury records.
- [ ] Report bleeding problems, sleep apnoea and CPAP use.
- [ ] Obtain medicine, fasting and postoperative nasal-care instructions.

### Admission day

- [ ] Bring identification, prescriptions, allergies and relevant reports.
- [ ] Confirm the exact procedure and responsible surgeon.
- [ ] Tell staff about new illness or accidental food and drink intake.
- [ ] Arrange the approved escort and transport home.
- [ ] Ask when splints, packing or follow-up will be reviewed.

### Before discharge

- [ ] Know what bleeding is expected and when to seek help.
- [ ] Understand prescribed sprays, rinses and pain relief.
- [ ] Obtain a contact number for concerns after hours.
- [ ] Confirm work, exercise, travel and review arrangements.

## Recovery and warning signs

Some discomfort and blood-stained discharge may occur, but your discharge instructions should distinguish expected changes from problems. Persistent heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty, faintness or rapidly worsening swelling needs urgent assessment. Increasing pain with fever, a worsening blockage or an offensive discharge should be reported promptly.

Do not assume that a painful blocked nose always represents routine swelling. The team may need to examine the septum or check for another complication. Avoid inserting objects into the nose or attempting to clear a clot yourself.

Keep follow-up appointments even if breathing improves. The surgeon can assess healing and discuss symptoms that remain. Allergy treatment may still be needed because correcting a septal deviation does not remove an allergic tendency.

## Common preparation mistakes

1. **Expecting a cosmetic change from isolated septoplasty.** Clarify the agreed aim.
2. **Assuming snoring will definitely stop.** Sleep-related symptoms need their own assessment.
3. **Using a sinus CT as a compulsory screening test.** Ask what question it answers.
4. **Removing a dressing because the nose feels blocked.** Contact the team instead.
5. **Restarting strenuous activity from someone else's timetable.** Follow your own operative plan.

## Questions for the ENT surgeon

1. What is causing my obstruction besides the septum?
2. What improvement is realistic after this operation?
3. Is another procedure being added, and why?
4. Will the outside shape of my nose change?
5. What anaesthesia is planned?
6. How should my sprays and other medicines be managed?
7. Will I have packing or splints?
8. What symptoms require urgent review?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does septoplasty treat allergies?

No. It addresses the septal structure. Allergy-related swelling may still require separate treatment.

### Will my nose look different?

Isolated septoplasty primarily treats the internal septum. Ask about any intended or possible external change and whether rhinoplasty is also planned.

### Is a CT scan always necessary?

No. It may be useful for suspected sinus disease or another specific question. The ENT examination guides the need for imaging.

### Should I stop nasal sprays beforehand?

Follow the surgeon's directions for each spray. Do not assume all sprays should be stopped or continued without checking.

### Why does the nose feel blocked after surgery?

Swelling, crusting and dressings can cause early blockage. The team should explain what is expected and which changes need examination.

### Can I remove the nasal packing myself?

Only follow explicit instructions for the material used. Many dressings and splints need professional assessment or planned removal.

### Can I use my CPAP as usual?

Ask the ENT and anaesthesia teams to coordinate a safe plan. Mask pressure and nasal healing can affect the advice.

### When can I return to work?

It depends on healing, the operation and exposure to dust, exertion or injury. Obtain a personalised return plan and any required certificate.

If your work requires a protective respirator, helmet or close-fitting mask, show the team the equipment before surgery. Ask when it can be worn comfortably and safely so a return-to-work certificate reflects your actual duties.

## Focus the plan on breathing and healing

A septoplasty surgery checklist should establish the cause of blockage, the exact procedure and the care needed afterwards. Confirm medicine instructions, arrange support and understand how to obtain help for bleeding or worsening pain. Evaluate the result after appropriate healing rather than during the first congested days.

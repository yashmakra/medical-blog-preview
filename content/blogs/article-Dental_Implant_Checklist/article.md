# Dental Implant Surgery Checklist: Bone Density Tests & Pre-Op Guidelines

A dental implant plan should begin with the tooth replacement you need, not simply a screw advertised at a discounted price. The dentist must assess the mouth, gum health, space for the restoration and the bone around nearby nerves or sinuses. The planned crown, bridge or denture should guide where an implant can sensibly be placed.

This dental implant surgery checklist explains the assessment behind treatment, including what “bone density tests” in an article title should mean in practice. Dental imaging may be needed to evaluate the implant site, but a whole-body bone-density scan is not a routine requirement for every implant patient. The clinical question should determine the investigation.

For patients in India, useful preparation also includes a written treatment sequence, clarity about who provides the final restoration and an estimate that distinguishes surgery from the later prosthetic work. Ask about alternatives, healing and maintenance before agreeing. The guide helps organise that discussion; it cannot determine whether an implant or bone graft is suitable for your mouth.

## Confirm the complete tooth-replacement plan

An implant is placed in the jawbone to support a replacement tooth or teeth. Treatment can involve several stages, with healing between them. Immediate placement after extraction and an immediately fitted tooth are separate decisions; neither is appropriate for every situation.

Discuss a bridge, removable denture or retaining a treatable tooth where relevant. Ask what the proposed restoration will do for chewing, appearance and cleaning. The [FDA implant information](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/dental-implants-what-you-should-know) explains that complications can arise early or later and may require additional treatment. An implant should not be sold as a maintenance-free lifetime replacement.

## Mouth, bone and medical assessment

Tell the dentist about gum disease, previous dental treatment, clenching, smoking or tobacco use, diabetes, delayed healing and any jaw problems. Bring earlier imaging when available. Explain medicines for osteoporosis or cancer, immune-suppressing treatment and previous radiotherapy to the head or neck, because these may affect planning.

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Dental and gum examination | Checks oral health and suitability of the proposed restoration | During treatment planning |
| Dental X-rays | Assesses teeth and available bone information | According to the site and clinical question |
| Selected three-dimensional imaging | Defines anatomy near nerves, sinuses or a complex site | When the dentist judges that the information is needed |
| Bite and restoration assessment | Plans implant position and loading | Before the surgical and prosthetic plan is final |
| Selected medical tests or consultation | Addresses diabetes, medicines or other health risks | When indicated by the history, not as a universal panel |

Ask how the image changes the treatment plan. A site assessment is different from a general osteoporosis investigation. If a bone graft or sinus-related procedure is proposed, clarify its purpose, alternatives and effect on the treatment sequence.

## Resolve active oral problems first

The team may recommend treating decay, gum inflammation or another dental infection before implantation. Ask what standard of cleaning is needed and how it will be reviewed. Smoking or tobacco use can affect healing; discuss support and expectations rather than concealing use.

Understand the temporary tooth arrangement while healing occurs. Ask whether a removable denture can be worn, whether pressure over the site must be avoided and how eating will be adapted. [Guy's and St Thomas' implant information](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/dental-implants/having-dental-implant) describes imaging and planning before implant placement.

## Medicines, local anaesthesia and sedation

Provide a complete prescription list, supplements, allergies and past anaesthesia reactions. Tell the team why blood thinners or bone-related medicines are prescribed. Do not stop them independently; the dentist may need to coordinate with your doctor before agreeing the operative plan.

Implant placement often uses local anaesthesia. Sedation or general anaesthesia may be considered for selected cases. Ask which applies, whether an escort is required and what food or drink instructions follow from that plan. Major-surgery fasting rules should not be assumed for a straightforward local-anaesthetic appointment.

Do not self-prescribe antibiotics or painkillers. Obtain the exact instructions for any prescribed medicine and what to do if a reaction or problem occurs. Explain diabetes and meal timing so the dental and medical teams can coordinate a safe appointment.

## Clarify fees, records and maintenance

Request a written sequence showing extraction if needed, grafting, implant placement, healing review and the final restoration. Ask who performs each stage and who manages a complication. A low implant-fixture price may exclude the abutment, crown, graft, scan or later visits.

Keep details of the implant system and the final restoration. These records can help if treatment continues elsewhere. Ask how cleaning around the implant will be taught and how often the dentist recommends review according to your risk. Daily care and professional maintenance remain important after the replacement tooth is fitted.

## Your dental implant preparation checklist

### Before agreeing to surgery

- [ ] Understand alternatives and the complete replacement-tooth plan.
- [ ] Review gum health, tobacco use and relevant medical conditions.
- [ ] Discuss site imaging and any need for a bone graft.
- [ ] Provide all medicines, especially blood thinners and bone-related treatment.
- [ ] Obtain a staged estimate including the final restoration.

### Before the appointment

- [ ] Follow the dentist's oral-care and medicine instructions.
- [ ] Confirm the anaesthesia, eating and escort arrangements.
- [ ] Carry ID, relevant prescriptions and previous imaging.
- [ ] Report swelling, fever or a new dental or medical problem.

### Before leaving

- [ ] Understand wound care, eating and temporary-tooth restrictions.
- [ ] Confirm review dates and who to call urgently.
- [ ] Ask when and how the implant records will be supplied.

## Recovery and warning signs

Ask what swelling, bruising or discomfort is expected for your procedure and how it should change. Follow instructions for cleaning, food texture and protection of the site. Do not press on the implant, repeatedly test its movement or use an unapproved product on the wound.

Contact the dentist promptly for increasing pain or swelling, persistent bleeding, fever, new or persistent numbness, pus or a loose implant or restoration. Rapid swelling affecting breathing or swallowing needs emergency assessment. The [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/dental-implants-what-you-should-know) advises reporting an implant that feels loose or painful rather than waiting for routine review.

## Five errors to avoid

1. **Comparing fixture prices instead of complete treatment.** Include the restoration and necessary preparatory work.
2. **Assuming every site needs a graft or full-body bone scan.** Ask which clinical question justifies it.
3. **Omitting bone-related medicines or radiotherapy history.** These can affect planning.
4. **Treating an implant as immune to gum problems.** Cleaning and review remain necessary.
5. **Chewing normally before clearance.** Follow the loading and temporary-restoration instructions.

## Questions for the implant clinician

1. Can this tooth be retained, or what alternatives exist?
2. How does the final restoration guide implant placement?
3. What imaging is necessary and why?
4. Is grafting needed, and what does it add to treatment?
5. Do my medicines or diabetes affect the plan?
6. What temporary tooth will I have while healing?
7. Who treats complications and supplies implant records?
8. What maintenance and future costs should I expect?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does everyone need a bone-density scan?

No. Implant-site imaging and a medical osteoporosis assessment answer different questions. The dentist selects investigations from the site and your health history.

### Can the implant and final tooth be fitted on the same day?

Sometimes a particular immediate approach is suitable, but stability, anatomy and the restoration plan matter. Ask what is temporary and what is final.

### Is bone grafting always necessary?

No. It depends on available bone and the planned restoration. Discuss the reason, alternatives and whether it changes healing stages.

### Can I have implants with diabetes?

Suitability depends on clinical assessment and disease control, among other factors. Bring relevant records and involve your doctor when the dentist requests it.

### Should I stop a blood thinner first?

Do not decide independently. The dental and prescribing teams should balance bleeding and clotting risks and give a specific plan.

### Is local anaesthesia enough?

It is commonly used, but the procedure and patient needs influence the plan. Ask about sedation, escort and eating instructions for your appointment.

### Will an implant last forever?

No guarantee is appropriate. Oral health, loading, maintenance and complications can affect the implant and restoration over time.

### Who should keep the implant details?

Ask for a copy and store it with your dental records. It can assist future maintenance, repairs or treatment by another dentist.

## Plan the restoration as well as surgery

A useful implant checklist connects healthy gums, appropriate imaging and medical disclosure with a clear restoration and maintenance plan. Understand the complete cost and sequence before treatment starts, and keep follow-up even after chewing feels comfortable.

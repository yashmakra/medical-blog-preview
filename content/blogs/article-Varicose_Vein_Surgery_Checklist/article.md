# Varicose Vein Surgery Checklist: Laser Treatment Prep & Diagnostic Doppler

Bulging leg veins can cause aching, heaviness, itching or skin problems, but their appearance does not reveal which veins are allowing blood to flow backwards. A good treatment decision therefore starts with a vascular assessment and, when indicated, a duplex ultrasound scan. The scan maps blood flow and helps distinguish a superficial-vein problem from other causes of leg symptoms.

This varicose vein surgery checklist explains what to prepare for laser or other endovenous treatment and when a different procedure may be discussed. It covers the Doppler examination, anaesthetic options, medicine questions, compression advice and practical arrangements for walking and travelling afterwards. Not everyone with visible veins needs an intervention, and laser is not the only treatment.

For patients comparing clinics in India, confirm who performs the scan, which exact vein is being treated and what the estimate includes. A photograph or online consultation alone cannot replace a physical and ultrasound assessment where needed. Report previous clots, ulcers, bleeding or pregnancy because these can change the discussion and the timing of treatment.

## What is being treated?

Veins return blood towards the heart. Faulty valves in some superficial veins can allow backward flow, called reflux, contributing to enlarged varicose veins. A vascular specialist should relate symptoms, examination and ultrasound findings before proposing treatment. Leg swelling can also have other causes; removing a visible vein does not automatically resolve every symptom.

The [NICE varicose-vein guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg168/resources/varicose-veins-diagnosis-and-management-pdf-35109698485957) recommends duplex ultrasound to establish reflux and plan intervention. For suitable truncal reflux, endothermal ablation is an option; foam treatment or surgery may be considered when it is unsuitable. Your anatomy and circumstances determine the recommendation.

Laser and radiofrequency treatments close a selected vein from within. Small surface veins may sometimes be treated separately. Ask whether all proposed components are necessary, whether both legs need treatment and whether procedures are planned together or in stages.

## Before the Doppler appointment

Describe when symptoms occur, whether standing worsens them and whether elevation helps. Mention a previous deep-vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, superficial thrombosis, pregnancy, leg injury, ulcer or earlier vein treatment. Bring previous scan reports if available.

Wear clothing that allows the legs to be examined without difficulty. Ask the clinic whether it has particular instructions for the scan. There is usually no reason to arrange a broad package of laboratory tests before a specialist has selected the treatment and anaesthesia.

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be needed |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical leg examination | Reviews veins, swelling, skin and ulcers | During specialist evaluation |
| Duplex ultrasound | Maps reflux and assesses relevant veins | To plan treatment for suspected primary or recurrent disease |
| Clot-risk assessment | Reviews previous clots and individual risk factors | Before intervention and travel advice |
| Arterial circulation assessment | Helps assess suitability for compression when circulation is a concern | According to symptoms and examination |
| Blood tests or ECG | Addresses a specific health or anaesthetic issue | Only when clinically indicated |

## Medicines, local anaesthesia and fasting

Tell the team about anticoagulants, antiplatelet medicines, hormone treatments, diabetes drugs and supplements. Explain why any blood thinner was prescribed. Do not stop it because a vein procedure looks minor; the clinician must weigh bleeding and clotting risks.

Endothermal treatment commonly uses local anaesthetic around the vein, while other procedures or patient circumstances may involve sedation or another anaesthetic. The [NHS varicose-vein overview](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/varicose-veins/) describes the broad treatment methods. Ask which method is being used for you and whether the amount of treatment changes the recovery arrangements.

Fasting instructions depend on this plan. Obtain them directly from the clinic, including whether normal food, clear drinks or regular medicines are permitted. Do not assume general-anaesthetic instructions apply to a local-only procedure, or that no fasting is needed if sedation has been proposed.

## Plan compression, walking and transport

Ask whether compression is recommended after your intervention, what size and pressure are prescribed, and how long to wear it. Stockings should not be bought solely from an online size chart when the team needs to assess fit or arterial circulation. Tell the clinician if you cannot put them on independently.

Arrange transport according to the anaesthesia and the clinic's discharge rules. Ask about walking, driving, prolonged sitting, air travel and return to standing work. These recommendations depend on the procedure and clot risk. A long bus journey home should be discussed before the treatment date, not at the exit desk.

The estimate should specify the vein and leg being treated, ultrasound, anaesthesia, additional surface-vein procedures, stockings and follow-up. Treatment of cosmetic concerns may be handled differently by insurers; confirm coverage using the clinical plan rather than a marketing package name.

If a leg ulcer is present, ask how wound care and compression will be coordinated with vein treatment. Bring details of dressings, allergies and previous wound infections. Clarify who will continue dressing changes after the procedure and how improvement will be assessed, rather than assuming closing a vein ends ulcer care immediately.

## Practical preparation checklist

### At the planning visit

- [ ] Ask which ultrasound finding explains the proposed treatment.
- [ ] Discuss alternatives and the possibility of recurrent or new veins.
- [ ] Report previous clots, vein procedures, ulcers and bleeding.
- [ ] Obtain a personalised medicine and anaesthesia plan.
- [ ] Clarify compression and travel advice.

### Before attending

- [ ] Confirm the exact procedure, leg and reporting location.
- [ ] Bring identification, prescriptions, scan results and insurance approval.
- [ ] Follow the clinic's skin-care and eating instructions.
- [ ] Avoid applying unrequested creams or shaving the treatment area yourself.
- [ ] Arrange approved transport and help with stockings if needed.

### Before leaving

- [ ] Understand the walking and activity plan.
- [ ] Know how to look after punctures or small incisions.
- [ ] Confirm whether a follow-up scan is planned.
- [ ] Obtain an urgent contact route for bleeding or clot symptoms.

## Recovery and symptoms that need attention

Bruising, tenderness or a pulling sensation can occur after vein treatment, but the team should explain what is expected for your procedure. Do not massage a newly painful swollen calf or assume it is normal healing. New one-sided swelling, marked calf pain, increasing redness, fever or worsening wound symptoms need prompt assessment.

Sudden breathlessness, chest pain, coughing blood or collapse needs emergency care. If a varicose vein bleeds, seek prompt medical help; heavy or persistent bleeding requires urgent attention. Ask your clinician for first-aid instructions tailored to your situation before discharge.

Continue follow-up even if the treated vein is no longer visible. Symptoms may come from more than one source, and additional or recurrent veins do not necessarily mean the first procedure was incorrectly performed. A review should reassess the cause before another intervention is purchased.

## Five mistakes to avoid

1. **Choosing treatment from photographs alone.** Ask for a proper reflux assessment.
2. **Equating every leg ache with varicose veins.** Other conditions may need evaluation.
3. **Stopping anticoagulation without advice.** Your original clot risk still matters.
4. **Buying compression without understanding fit and suitability.** Confirm the prescription.
5. **Planning immediate long-distance travel.** Discuss the route and clot risk first.

## Questions for your vascular specialist

1. Which vein has reflux, and does it explain my symptoms?
2. Why is laser, radiofrequency, foam or surgery recommended?
3. Will surface veins need additional treatment?
4. What are my individual clot and bleeding risks?
5. What anaesthesia is planned?
6. What compression and walking advice applies?
7. When should I return for review or ultrasound?
8. What happens if symptoms persist or new veins appear?

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all visible varicose veins need surgery?

No. Symptoms, skin changes, complications and patient preferences influence the decision. A vascular assessment establishes whether intervention is appropriate.

### Is Doppler the same as duplex ultrasound?

Clinics may use “Doppler” informally for a duplex blood-flow scan. Ask whether the examination maps reflux and the relevant vein anatomy for treatment planning.

### Is laser always better than other options?

No. Anatomy, previous treatment and clinical suitability affect the choice. Ask why the recommended method fits your situation.

### Will I need general anaesthesia?

Not always. Many endovenous treatments use local anaesthesia, but sedation or other techniques may be used in selected circumstances.

### Should I buy stockings before the appointment?

Wait for advice on suitability and fit. A stocking that is difficult to apply or incorrectly sized may not meet the intended purpose.

### Can veins return after treatment?

Yes. New or recurrent varicose veins can develop. Ask how the proposed treatment addresses your current reflux and what follow-up is recommended.

### Can I stand at work the following day?

Your job, symptoms and procedure matter. Obtain a plan for walking, breaks and return to prolonged standing rather than relying on a universal promise.

### Does a painless swollen leg need review?

Yes, especially if the swelling is new or one-sided. A clot or another problem can occur without severe pain and should not be assessed from appearance alone.

## Make the scan guide the plan

A varicose vein surgery checklist is most useful when it connects symptoms with ultrasound findings and an individual treatment plan. Confirm the target vein, medicines, compression, travel and follow-up before paying for a procedure. Clear preparation matters more than the name of the technology used.

# Top 10 Hospitals for Heart Surgery in Mumbai: Cardiac Care Comparison

A recommendation for heart surgery can arrive after months of symptoms or suddenly after an investigation. Either way, families often begin by searching for a famous hospital before they fully understand the operation. A useful comparison of the top hospitals for heart surgery Mumbai offers begins with a clearer question: is the proposed treatment coronary bypass, valve repair or replacement, aortic surgery, congenital-heart surgery or something else?

Those operations have different indications, risks and recovery needs. A cardiology department alone does not establish that a hospital performs your required surgery. The relevant comparison is the surgical team’s current practice, supporting cardiac services and the plan for your health conditions. Your cardiologist and cardiac surgeon should explain why an operation is being considered and which alternatives remain reasonable.

This guide identifies ten Mumbai hospitals with official cardiac-surgery evidence. They appear in no performance order. It helps you organise consultations, compare written plans and prepare financial questions; it cannot establish which hospital will produce the best outcome for an individual patient. For an unstable or emergency condition, seek immediate medical care instead of delaying treatment to complete a shortlist.

## A shortlist based on services, not awards

We checked official hospital pages, service profiles and hospital-hosted brochures on 28 October 2026. Each entry identifies a Mumbai location and a cardiac-surgical service. Provider descriptions are useful evidence of advertised capability, but do not independently verify quality, the availability of a particular surgeon or the results of every operation.

No scores have been assigned. We have not independently audited surgical mortality, stroke, infection or readmission figures, and we do not assert current accreditation status. Ask for the current certificate and scope for the exact campus if required. A hospital should also explain whether quoted outcomes concern your operation, your risk group and a recent reporting period.

## Understand the proposed operation first

Coronary artery bypass grafting creates a route for blood to bypass narrowed coronary arteries. It is different from an angioplasty procedure performed through a catheter. The NHS explains that the purpose is to improve blood supply to the heart; whether bypass is appropriate depends on the individual assessment. Ask your treating team to explain the coronary findings and the options in your circumstances.

For a valve problem, ask which valve is affected, the severity of disease and whether repair or replacement is being considered. If replacement is proposed, discuss valve choices, their implications and the future follow-up required. Do not select a valve from a package brochure. Your team should explain the trade-offs in relation to age, health, preferences and the practical ability to follow treatment.

A smaller incision is an access method, not a separate diagnosis. Ask whether it is suitable for the actual operation, what could lead to a change in approach and how that possibility is included in consent. If you are told that surgery is urgent, ask what makes delay unsafe and what needs to happen next. Seeking a second opinion should be coordinated with the treating team when time is clinically limited.

## How to ask about the team and safety

Request a consultation with the cardiac surgeon who is expected to operate. Take your medication list, previous procedure notes, angiography images, echocardiography and relevant blood tests. A second opinion is more useful when both teams review the same information rather than starting from an incomplete verbal summary.

Ask who provides cardiac anaesthesia and intensive care, how other medical conditions are assessed and whether the proposed operation needs any additional specialist input. Clarify the plan if the operation changes after direct inspection or if a complication requires another procedure. These questions do not predict an adverse event; they establish how decisions will be communicated.

Identify one relative as the principal contact and ask how updates are provided during surgery and ICU care. After discharge, request a named service or telephone route for clinical questions, the review schedule and rehabilitation instructions. People travelling from outside Mumbai should discuss whether they need to remain nearby before returning home.

## Costs: request a cardiac-specific estimate

No universal Mumbai heart-surgery price is quoted here because current, comparable, complete tariffs were not established. A bypass package and a combined valve-and-bypass operation are not interchangeable. Even estimates for the same named operation may differ in room category, consumables, investigations and the care anticipated for a particular patient.

Ask for the proposed procedure, surgeon and anaesthetist fees, theatre charges, ICU allowance, room charges, medicines, blood-related charges where applicable, devices or valve implants, investigations and initial follow-up. Request a clear list of exclusions and the charging basis if ICU or ward stay extends. Ask whether rehabilitation and visits after discharge are included or billed separately.

For insurance, confirm the exact hospital’s network status, pre-authorisation requirements, room entitlement, co-payment and non-payable items with the insurer or TPA. A cashless approval is not necessarily an undertaking to pay every charge. Ask how the deposit and settlement work if approval is delayed. Preserve the dated estimate and note who will authorise additional expenses.

## Read success claims with care

“Success” can refer to surviving admission, relief of symptoms, avoiding complications or longer-term health. A single percentage without a definition cannot compare hospitals fairly. Ask for a recent denominator, the operation concerned, the period measured and whether the figures account for differences in patient risk. Emergency surgery and elective surgery should not be combined casually.

An individual surgeon’s stated experience and a hospital’s marketing award do not replace an audited outcome report. Patient testimonials can reveal communication and billing experiences, but cannot establish comparative clinical safety. Discuss your own estimated risks, expected benefits and uncertainty during the consent conversation. A credible discussion should allow questions and explain both benefits and possible complications.

## Practical questions for the consultation

1. What diagnosis makes surgery appropriate now?
2. What would happen if I chose another reasonable treatment?
3. What exact operation is planned, and could it change?
4. Who will operate and who provides cardiac anaesthesia?
5. What health issues should be addressed before admission?
6. Which charges are excluded from the estimate?
7. How will the family receive updates?
8. What support is needed at home and in rehabilitation?

Write the answers down. If two hospitals recommend different procedures, ask each team to identify the clinical finding driving its recommendation. A meaningful second opinion explains the difference rather than simply offering a cheaper package for an operation that may not be equivalent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the best-known cardiac hospital always the right choice?

Reputation can help identify options, but your diagnosis, the relevant surgical team and the practical care pathway matter. Ask about experience with the specific operation and the support your medical conditions require.

### Does this list rank hospitals by heart-surgery success?

No. Comparable, independently audited, risk-adjusted results were not established. The list verifies an advertised surgical service and location, then provides questions for individual assessment.

### Can I compare a bypass quote with an angioplasty quote?

They describe different procedures. First ask why each is being considered for your coronary anatomy and circumstances. Compare financial proposals only after understanding the clinical alternatives.

### Does every valve problem need replacement?

The plan depends on the valve disease and your assessment. Ask whether monitoring, medicines, repair, replacement or a catheter-based approach is relevant. A package name cannot determine the appropriate option.

### Should a minimally invasive approach decide the hospital?

It is one discussion point. Ask why the approach suits your case, who performs it and how the operation would proceed if a wider exposure became necessary. Do not treat incision size as an outcome guarantee.

### What should I send for a second opinion?

Provide original imaging where available, reports, previous intervention or operation records, current medicines and relevant medical history. Confirm whether the surgeon needs an in-person examination before a definitive plan.

### Will insurance cover the full admission?

Coverage depends on the policy, authorisation and actual charges. Verify procedure approval, room limits, co-payment and exclusions with the insurer as well as the hospital. Obtain a written explanation of likely personal expenses.

### Can I return outside Mumbai immediately after discharge?

Ask the treating team about fitness to travel, review needs and access to help at your destination. Discharge from hospital and suitability for a long journey are separate assessments.

### Who should explain medicines after the operation?

Request a reconciled discharge prescription and instructions from the treating team. Clarify dose changes, monitoring and whom to contact with problems. Do not stop or copy another patient’s cardiac medicines independently.

# Carpal Tunnel Surgery Checklist: Diagnostic Tests & Hand Surgery Preparation

Carpal tunnel symptoms often disturb sleep before they interfere with daytime tasks. Tingling or numbness in the thumb and nearby fingers, dropping objects or difficulty with fine movements may prompt a hand assessment. These symptoms can have other causes, however, so the decision to operate should be linked to the examination and the pattern of nerve compression rather than wrist pain alone.

This carpal tunnel surgery checklist explains how to prepare for decompression, including when nerve tests are useful, what local anaesthesia means and how to organise everyday tasks while the hand heals. The aim of surgery is to release pressure on the median nerve. Long-standing numbness or muscle weakness may not recover completely, and improvement can occur at different speeds.

For patients in India, practical preparation includes confirming which hand is being treated, whether surgery is planned on one or both sides, and how transport and work will be managed. Bring previous nerve-test results and describe your actual job duties. A short procedure can still require thoughtful preparation for dressing, cooking and lifting afterwards.

## Confirm the diagnosis before booking decompression

The median nerve travels through a narrow passage at the wrist beneath a strong ligament. Carpal tunnel release divides that ligament to create more room. Open and endoscopic approaches use different access methods; the surgeon should explain the choice, relevant risks and alternatives.

Symptoms, examination and clinical assessment can establish the diagnosis in many patients. The [AAOS 2024 guideline](https://www.aaos.org/quality/quality-programs/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/?tab=all_guidelines) replaced its 2016 edition and supports structured clinical diagnosis rather than routine advanced imaging for everyone. Nerve-conduction testing may still be appropriate when the diagnosis, severity or another nerve disorder needs clarification.

Discuss whether splinting, an injection or another non-operative approach remains reasonable. Surgery may be considered for persistent symptoms, significant compression or progression. Tell the clinician about constant numbness, weakness and loss of thumb-muscle bulk, rather than describing only the pain level.

## Tests and health information to bring

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be useful |
|---|---|---|
| Hand and nerve examination | Relates symptoms to median-nerve compression | At the specialist consultation |
| Nerve-conduction studies | Clarifies nerve function and diagnostic uncertainty | When the result would change management |
| Ultrasound | Answers a selected structural or diagnostic question | When indicated by the specialist |
| Diabetes or other relevant health review | Identifies conditions influencing symptoms and recovery | According to history |
| Anaesthetic assessment and selected blood tests | Plans the proposed technique safely | Based on health and procedural needs |

An MRI is not automatically required for carpal tunnel diagnosis. Bring reports already obtained so the surgeon can decide whether anything needs repeating. Mention symptoms in the neck, the other hand or the little finger because they may suggest additional problems needing assessment.

## Medicines, anaesthesia and eating instructions

Many carpal tunnel operations can use local anaesthesia. Sedation or another method may be considered in particular circumstances. Ask whether you will be fully awake and how discomfort or anxiety will be managed. The [NHS explanation](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/) describes local-anaesthetic release, but your hospital must confirm its own plan.

Provide a complete list of prescribed medicines, anticoagulants, diabetes treatments, supplements and allergies. Do not independently stop aspirin or another blood thinner. Ask which medicine instructions apply to the actual anaesthetic technique rather than assuming that a small incision needs no review.

Fasting may differ between a local-only procedure and one involving sedation. Obtain clear instructions directly from the team. Tell staff about fever, a skin infection or a wound on the proposed hand before admission.

## Organise the hand-dependent parts of daily life

Identify the tasks that will be difficult with a dressing or temporary discomfort: opening containers, washing, fastening clothes, using walking aids or caring for a child. Ask whether someone should help with meals and heavier chores. If both hands are affected, discuss the practical trade-offs of staged versus simultaneous treatment.

Tell the surgeon about typing, tools, vibration, heavy gripping and repetitive work. A return-to-work plan should reflect these tasks rather than the job title alone. Ask for advice about driving, lifting and wound protection, and arrange transport after the procedure according to the clinic's rules.

If you depend on a stick, crutches or a walker, demonstrate how much weight the hand normally supports. Temporary discomfort or a dressing can affect safe mobility even when the operation is small. Ask whether an alternative aid or additional help is needed. The same discussion matters for someone who transfers from a wheelchair, works with tools or provides physical care to another person; these needs should shape the timing and support plan.

## Checklist for carpal tunnel release

### Before attending

- [ ] Confirm the diagnosis, proposed hand and reason for surgery.
- [ ] Discuss alternatives and realistic recovery of sensation or strength.
- [ ] Bring nerve-test results and your medicine list.
- [ ] Report skin infection, injury or new illness.
- [ ] Clarify fasting, anaesthesia and transport requirements.
- [ ] Arrange help with hand-dependent household tasks.

### At the hospital

- [ ] Carry identification, insurance details and relevant reports.
- [ ] Participate in checking the correct hand and consent.
- [ ] Remove jewellery as instructed before swelling or a dressing makes this difficult.
- [ ] Tell the team about any changed medicine doses or food intake.

### Before going home

- [ ] Understand dressing care, permitted movement and pain relief.
- [ ] Know when the wound or sutures will be reviewed.
- [ ] Obtain guidance for work and driving.
- [ ] Know whom to contact about increasing pain or numbness.

## Recovery and warning signs

The dressing, wound and nerve may each recover on a different timetable. Night symptoms may improve before strength or persistent numbness. Ask what improvement is realistic if compression has been longstanding, and when a lack of progress should trigger reassessment.

Follow the team's instructions for finger movement, wound protection and any hand therapy. Do not force gripping exercises or soak the dressing without permission. A prescribed rehabilitation plan is different from exercises found in an online video.

Increasing redness, discharge, fever, severe pain, a very tight dressing or fingers that become cold or discoloured needs prompt advice. New major weakness or rapidly worsening symptoms should be assessed rather than waiting for the routine appointment.

## Five mistakes to avoid

1. **Assuming all wrist tingling is carpal tunnel syndrome.** Confirm the nerve diagnosis.
2. **Buying an MRI before specialist assessment.** Ask whether imaging changes the decision.
3. **Expecting immediate recovery of longstanding weakness.** Discuss nerve damage and realistic goals.
4. **Scheduling heavy work without a recovery plan.** Describe actual gripping and lifting demands.
5. **Removing or tightening a dressing yourself when symptoms worsen.** Contact the treating unit.

## Questions for the hand specialist

1. How certain is the diagnosis?
2. Do nerve tests change the recommendation?
3. Are there reasonable alternatives to surgery now?
4. Why is an open or endoscopic approach proposed?
5. What recovery is realistic for my numbness and strength?
6. What anaesthesia and fasting instructions apply?
7. How will work, driving and walking aids be managed?
8. When should the wound and nerve symptoms be reviewed?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every patient need nerve-conduction testing?

No. Testing is selected when it clarifies diagnosis or management. A structured clinical assessment can be sufficient in some cases.

### Is an MRI compulsory before surgery?

No. Routine MRI is not the standard requirement for diagnosing carpal tunnel syndrome. Ask what specific question any scan would answer.

### Can surgery be done under local anaesthesia?

Often it can. Confirm whether sedation or another technique is also planned because this affects preparation and transport.

### Will numbness disappear immediately?

Not always. Nerve recovery depends partly on the severity and duration of compression. Some deficits may persist despite adequate release.

### Can both hands be treated together?

Sometimes, but temporary limitations can be difficult. Discuss self-care, support and the advantages of staging treatment.

### Will I need physiotherapy?

Not every patient needs supervised therapy. Your surgeon should explain movement advice and whether a particular problem warrants hand-therapy input.

### When can I type or use tools?

The answer depends on comfort, wound healing and task demands. Heavy gripping and vibration need a different plan from light keyboard use.

### What if symptoms remain after surgery?

Attend follow-up. Persistent symptoms may reflect nerve recovery, longstanding damage or another diagnosis that needs reassessment.

Ask whether your workplace can temporarily modify gripping, vibration or manual-load tasks. A written description of duties helps the clinician give more useful advice than a certificate based only on the label “office” or “manual” work.

## Prepare for function as well as the procedure

A carpal tunnel surgery checklist should connect a clear diagnosis with realistic nerve recovery and manageable daily tasks. Confirm the correct hand, medicine and anaesthetic instructions, then arrange practical support and wound follow-up. Report deterioration rather than waiting simply because the incision is small.

# Bladder Tumour Surgery Checklist: Preoperative Clearances Before TURBT

A bladder growth seen during cystoscopy may lead to a transurethral resection of bladder tumour, usually shortened to TURBT. The operation can remove visible tumour and obtain tissue that establishes the diagnosis, grade and depth of disease. Those laboratory findings often determine what treatment is needed next, so preparation includes planning for the result appointment as well as the day in theatre.

This bladder tumour surgery checklist explains the assessment before TURBT, urine and medicine questions, anaesthetic options and the possibility of a temporary urinary catheter. It also explains why some patients need another resection or treatment inside the bladder afterwards. TURBT is not the same as removing the entire bladder, and it should not be assumed to complete treatment for every type of bladder cancer.

For patients in India, carry the cystoscopy findings, imaging and prescriptions, and clarify where pathology will be processed and discussed. Tell the team about infection symptoms, blood thinners and difficulty passing urine. Heavy bleeding with clots, retention or severe illness warrants urgent assessment rather than waiting for a planned admission.

## What TURBT is intended to establish

The surgeon passes an instrument through the urethra to inspect and remove selected abnormal tissue inside the bladder. There is usually no external abdominal incision. Tissue is sent to pathology, which helps distinguish non-muscle-invasive from muscle-invasive disease and identifies features that influence further care.

The visible size of a growth does not reliably establish its stage or grade. Ask what is known from cystoscopy and what can only be clarified after resection. The [NCI bladder-cancer summary](https://www.cancer.gov/types/bladder/hp/bladder-treatment-pdq) explains the role of transurethral resection in diagnosis and treatment.

Depending on the findings, the next step may involve surveillance, another TURBT, intravesical treatment or a different cancer pathway. “All visible tumour removed” is not the same statement as “no future treatment or checks are needed”.

## Preoperative evaluation and records

Describe the pattern of bleeding, clots, burning urine, fever and any difficulty emptying the bladder. Mention previous bladder tumours, prostate or urethral procedures, urinary infections and reactions to antibiotics. Bring earlier pathology if this is a repeat procedure.

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Cystoscopy findings | Locates and describes visible bladder abnormalities | In diagnosis and planning |
| Urine testing or culture | Checks suspected infection and informs treatment | According to symptoms and local preoperative protocol |
| CT or MRI | Assesses relevant urinary anatomy or suspected invasive disease | When findings would change staging or management |
| Blood count | Assesses anaemia where bleeding or other factors justify it | As directed by the team |
| Kidney function and electrolytes | Informs contrast, medicine and anaesthesia planning | According to health and investigations |
| Anaesthetic assessment | Selects an appropriate technique and monitoring | Before the procedure |

The [NICE bladder-cancer guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng2/chapter/Recommendations) discusses staging imaging when invasive disease is suspected and the importance of adequate tissue for assessment. It does not make every scan necessary for every person with a bladder lesion.

## Medicines, infection and anaesthesia

Give the team a complete list of anticoagulants, antiplatelets, diabetes treatments, pain medicines and supplements. Include why blood thinners were prescribed and any coronary stent or previous clot. The surgeon and prescribing clinician must agree how they are managed; do not use a fixed internet stop interval.

Report fever, rigors, burning urine or a new antibiotic prescription before surgery. The team decides whether urine testing, treatment or a change of timing is needed. Do not take leftover antibiotics to make a urine result appear normal.

TURBT may use general or spinal anaesthesia. Discuss previous anaesthetic problems, back procedures, breathing difficulties and preferences. A spinal technique is not automatically suitable for everyone, particularly where medicines or other clinical factors affect its safety.

Ask for written fasting and diabetes-medicine instructions. If you cannot follow them or a dose is missed, tell the team. Do not increase fluid intake indiscriminately if you have heart or kidney restrictions; ask how hydration should be handled around the procedure.

## Ask about catheter care and immediate bladder treatment

A catheter may be left temporarily to drain urine. Some patients need bladder irrigation or observation for bleeding. Ask whether going home with a catheter is possible and how removal or a trial of urination would be arranged. The [Leeds TURBT information](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/t-u-r-b-t-trans-urethral-resection-of-bladder-tumour/) describes these practical aspects; medicine instructions must still be personalised.

An immediate dose of chemotherapy inside the bladder may be considered in appropriate circumstances. It is not the same as intravenous chemotherapy and is not safe or suitable in every operative situation. Ask whether it is planned, what could prevent it being given and what handling precautions apply afterwards.

Before discharge, understand what urine colour and discomfort the team expects and how to recognise blocked drainage. If a catheter is present, obtain a demonstration and the contact number for problems. Do not remove or flush it yourself unless a clinician has specifically trained and instructed you.

## Make the result appointment workable

Before paying for admission, ask how pathology, follow-up cystoscopy and any additional treatment are charged. The surgical package may not cover the complete diagnostic and surveillance pathway. If another hospital will provide continuing care, request copies of the operative findings and pathology and identify the receiving clinician. Keep the original tissue-access details where available; a specialist review may require more than a photographed report. Ask whom to contact if the result appointment is delayed, rather than assuming no news means no further care is needed.

## TURBT preparation checklist

### Before admission

- [ ] Know the purpose of the operation and what pathology will clarify.
- [ ] Bring cystoscopy, imaging and previous bladder pathology records.
- [ ] Report urinary infection symptoms, heavy bleeding or retention.
- [ ] Obtain a written blood-thinner and diabetes-medicine plan.
- [ ] Clarify anaesthesia, fasting and expected admission arrangements.
- [ ] Arrange transport and help if a catheter or anaesthesia limits independence.

### On the day

- [ ] Carry identification, prescriptions, allergies and insurance details.
- [ ] Tell staff the last medicine doses and any food or drink intake.
- [ ] Confirm consent for resection and any proposed bladder instillation.
- [ ] Ask who will update your caregiver after the procedure.

### Before discharge

- [ ] Understand urine, catheter and activity instructions.
- [ ] Know the plan for restarting relevant medicines.
- [ ] Confirm the pathology-result appointment.
- [ ] Obtain urgent advice instructions for clots, retention, fever or worsening pain.
- [ ] Ask whether further resection or surveillance may be needed.

## Results and follow-up matter as much as removal

The pathology report describes the tissue type, grade and depth of tumour and whether the sample provides the information needed. Some patients require another TURBT because of risk features, incomplete removal or the need for better staging. The [EAU diagnostic guidance](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-muscleinvasive-bladder-cancer/chapter/diagnosis) explains these purposes of repeat resection.

Ask when the report will be ready and who will explain it. Request a copy and a written next-step plan. A scheduled result discussion should not be cancelled simply because blood in the urine has settled.

If bladder cancer is confirmed, surveillance and additional treatment are chosen according to risk. Follow-up is not interchangeable between low-risk and high-risk disease, and a normal urine test cannot automatically replace a planned cystoscopy.

## Recovery and symptoms requiring help

Some irritation or blood-stained urine may occur, but the discharge team should explain the expected pattern and activity restrictions. Persistent or heavy bleeding, large clots, inability to pass urine, a catheter that stops draining, fever or worsening lower-abdominal pain needs prompt assessment.

Severe weakness, fainting, breathlessness or uncontrolled bleeding requires emergency care. Do not attempt to manage retention by drinking increasingly large amounts while waiting for the bladder to empty. Contact the treating unit or an emergency service.

Ask about returning to work, exercise, sex and travel based on the resection and symptoms. If you will be away from the operating centre, know where catheter or bleeding problems can be assessed locally.

## Five mistakes to avoid

1. **Assuming TURBT removes the whole bladder.** Understand the actual procedure.
2. **Treating a small visible growth as automatically low risk.** Pathology determines important details.
3. **Stopping a blood thinner without coordination.** The underlying reason for treatment matters.
4. **Ignoring blocked catheter drainage.** Retention needs timely assessment.
5. **Missing the pathology appointment when bleeding improves.** The next treatment decision still needs review.

## Questions for the urological oncologist

1. What did cystoscopy show, and what remains uncertain?
2. Is additional imaging needed before resection?
3. What anaesthesia is suitable for me?
4. How will blood thinners be managed?
5. Might I need a catheter at home?
6. Is a bladder instillation planned after resection?
7. When will pathology be discussed?
8. What could make a second TURBT necessary?
9. What follow-up is likely if cancer is confirmed?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a bladder tumour always mean cancer?

Not every abnormality has the same diagnosis. Tissue assessment establishes what the growth is and guides further treatment.

### Does TURBT remove my bladder?

No. It removes selected tissue from inside the bladder through the urethra. Radical cystectomy is a different operation.

### Will I definitely need a catheter?

It may be used depending on the resection and bleeding. Ask about the expected plan and how removal is organised.

### Can I take my blood thinner as usual?

Follow the coordinated plan from your team. Neither stopping nor continuing it should be assumed safe without considering your circumstances.

### Why might a second TURBT be recommended?

It may help clear remaining tumour or provide more reliable staging information. The reason should be explained using your pathology and operative findings.

### Is chemotherapy inside the bladder compulsory?

No. Suitability depends on the clinical and operative situation. Ask about the intended benefit and any reasons it might be withheld.

### Can a normal urine test replace cystoscopy later?

Not automatically. Surveillance depends on the diagnosis and risk. Follow the schedule agreed with the urology team.

### What if I cannot pass urine after discharge?

Seek urgent assessment. Do not wait until the next clinic appointment or attempt to solve retention by forcing fluids.

If another family member manages your appointments, give them the result date and contact details with your permission. A clear reminder can help avoid an important pathology discussion being lost among catheter or recovery visits.

## Prepare for the result, not only the procedure

A bladder tumour surgery checklist should protect safe resection and the decisions that follow it. Confirm medicine instructions, learn catheter care if needed and keep the pathology appointment. Understanding the next step is essential even when the immediate recovery seems straightforward.

# LASIK Eye Surgery Checklist: Screening Tests & Laser Eye Prep Guidelines

Wanting freedom from spectacles is a reasonable reason to explore LASIK, but a clear distance prescription does not establish that the operation is safe for your eyes. The assessment must consider the cornea's shape and thickness, dry-eye symptoms, prescription stability and expectations about night vision and future reading glasses. A low advertised price cannot answer those questions.

This LASIK eye surgery checklist helps you prepare for the screening appointment and understand what should happen before treatment is agreed. It covers contact-lens instructions, the examination, medicine and health information to disclose, transport and early recovery. It is written for patients comparing refractive services in India, where package names may make different techniques sound interchangeable.

Ask the surgeon to explain whether LASIK is suitable, whether another correction method is preferable and what limitations remain even after a technically successful procedure. Do not feel pressured to decide immediately after testing. Keep the written information and bring questions back. This guide supports that discussion; it cannot determine candidacy or replace instructions for your eyes and the device being used.

## What LASIK changes

LASIK reshapes corneal tissue to change the eye's focusing power. A thin flap is created, treatment is applied beneath it and the flap is repositioned. Other refractive procedures use different methods, so ask for the exact name rather than accepting “laser eye surgery” as a complete description.

Glasses, contact lenses or another procedure may remain reasonable options. Ask how the expected benefit compares with dry-eye symptoms, glare, halos, under- or over-correction and other risks. LASIK does not prevent all later eye disease or the natural age-related need for reading correction. The [FDA's overview](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/what-lasik) explains the basic mechanism.

## Screening should establish suitability

Explain changes in your prescription, eye rubbing, previous eye injury or surgery, infection, dry eye, general health and medicines. Discuss pregnancy or breastfeeding where relevant, autoimmune conditions and any treatment that could affect healing or measurements. The clinician needs this information before an eligibility decision.

| Assessment | Purpose | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Refraction and prescription history | Checks the correction and its stability | During baseline assessment |
| Corneal topography or tomography | Evaluates corneal shape and possible irregularity | As part of suitability planning |
| Corneal thickness measurement | Helps assess the tissue available for treatment | Before a corneal laser plan is agreed |
| Tear-film and ocular-surface assessment | Investigates dryness or inflammation | Screening and whenever symptoms are present |
| Eye examination, including other structures | Identifies disease that may affect treatment or vision | According to the ophthalmologist's assessment |

The [FDA candidacy guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/when-lasik-not-me) highlights situations requiring caution. Ask for the findings from your own eyes: a screening appointment should lead to an explanation, not simply a package offer.

## Contact lenses can affect measurements

Tell the clinic the type of lenses you wear and how often. Lenses can temporarily alter corneal shape, so the surgeon may require time without them before testing and may repeat measurements. Obtain the interval for your lens type and eyes; do not assume a friend's instructions are adequate.

Use spectacles as directed during this period and bring the relevant prescriptions. If you accidentally wear lenses again, tell the clinic before measurements are relied upon. Rechecking stability is preferable to building a treatment plan from inaccurate information.

## Understand consent before booking

Ask which surgeon will assess and treat you and what experience they have with your correction and the planned system. Discuss the chance that spectacles, contact lenses or another intervention may still be needed afterwards. No provider can responsibly promise perfect vision in every situation or freedom from every visual symptom.

If monovision is proposed, ask how the difference between the eyes will affect your tasks and whether a trial is suitable. Night driving, detailed work and contact sports deserve specific discussion. Ask how complications and enhancements are assessed and charged, rather than assuming all further care is included.

## Medicines, numbing drops and eating instructions

LASIK is usually performed with local anaesthetic eye drops, sometimes with additional medicine for relaxation. It does not routinely involve the same anaesthetic pathway as a major inpatient operation. The clinic must explain any sedation, food or drink instructions and escort requirements.

Give the ophthalmologist your prescription and non-prescription medicines, eye drops, supplements and allergies. Do not start antibiotic or steroid drops yourself, and do not stop systemic treatment independently. If the eye surface needs treatment before the final assessment, ask when measurements should be repeated.

The [FDA preparation guide](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/what-should-i-expect-during-and-after-surgery) advises discussing risks and expectations, contact-lens use and transport. Follow the clinic's directions about makeup, creams and other products around the eyes on treatment day.

## Arrange the practical details

Plan transport to treatment and the early review; do not assume you can drive because the procedure is short. Bring identification, previous prescriptions, screening records and the clinic's written instructions. Ask whether someone should stay with you and how to reach the surgeon if symptoms worsen that evening.

For work, discuss screen comfort, breaks, air conditioning, dust exposure and protective equipment. Arrange the first review before making travel commitments. If you live elsewhere, ask which later visits need the operating clinic and how urgent problems will be assessed near home.

If night driving is essential for your job, raise it during consent rather than after treatment. Ask how glare or fluctuating vision would affect your work plan and when the surgeon will reassess those tasks.

## LASIK preparation checklist

### Before screening

- [ ] Give the clinic your contact-lens type and prescription history.
- [ ] Follow the prescribed lens-free interval before measurements.
- [ ] Report dry eye, previous eye surgery and relevant health conditions.
- [ ] List eye drops, systemic medicines and allergies.

### Before treatment

- [ ] Understand your corneal findings and why LASIK is or is not appropriate.
- [ ] Compare alternatives and discuss night-vision and reading expectations.
- [ ] Obtain instructions for products around the eyes and any prescribed drops.
- [ ] Confirm escort, transport, early review and urgent contact arrangements.
- [ ] Check the estimate, follow-up coverage and enhancement policy.

### After the procedure

- [ ] Use prescribed drops and protection exactly as instructed.
- [ ] Avoid rubbing the eyes and follow activity restrictions.
- [ ] Attend review even if vision seems satisfactory.

## Recovery and signs needing urgent review

Some blur, discomfort, watering or light sensitivity may occur, but the clinic should explain what is expected and how symptoms should evolve. Vision may fluctuate. Ask when driving, swimming, makeup, exercise and dusty work can resume rather than treating an online timetable as clearance.

Contact the eye surgeon promptly for severe pain, deteriorating vision or symptoms that worsen rather than improve. Sudden loss of vision or another serious eye concern needs urgent assessment. Do not keep wearing a contact lens in the treated eye or add old prescription drops to manage an unexpected problem without medical advice.

## Five errors to avoid

1. **Choosing from the spectacle prescription alone.** Corneal and ocular-surface findings matter.
2. **Hiding recent contact-lens wear.** It can affect measurements and planning.
3. **Accepting a perfect-vision guarantee.** Discuss realistic benefits and limitations.
4. **Driving yourself home.** Arrange transport and early review.
5. **Ignoring worsening symptoms between visits.** Contact the treating clinic promptly.

## Questions for your refractive surgeon

1. What do my corneal shape and thickness measurements show?
2. Is my prescription stable enough for the proposed treatment?
3. Does dry eye need treatment before measurements are finalised?
4. Why recommend LASIK rather than another option?
5. How might treatment affect night driving and reading?
6. What is the plan if my correction or symptoms are unsatisfactory?
7. Which follow-up visits and possible treatments are included?
8. How do I obtain urgent help outside clinic hours?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a free screening enough to confirm eligibility?

Only if it includes the necessary clinical assessment and a surgeon's explanation. Ask which tests were performed, what they show and whether additional evaluation is required.

### Must everyone stop lenses for the same period?

No. Lens type, wear history and corneal response influence the instructions. The clinic may repeat measurements to confirm stability before proceeding.

### Can LASIK cure dry eye?

It is not a treatment for dry eye and may worsen dryness. The ocular surface needs assessment, appropriate treatment and a candidacy discussion before surgery.

### Will I never need reading glasses?

No such promise is appropriate. Age-related focusing changes can still occur, and some people need correction for particular tasks even after treatment.

### Is “all-laser” treatment automatically the best choice?

The term describes aspects of the technique, not universal suitability. Ask how it relates to your corneal findings, expected correction and alternatives.

### Is general anaesthesia normally necessary?

LASIK commonly uses numbing drops. Ask about any relaxation medicine or sedation and follow the clinic's corresponding food, transport and supervision instructions.

### Can I postpone treatment after the assessment?

Yes, an elective decision should allow time for informed consideration. Ask whether measurements or health checks would need repeating if you return later.

### Can I skip review if I can already see well?

No. Review checks healing and eye health, not just perceived sharpness. Attend the agreed visits and report new symptoms between them.

## Choose from the examination findings

A useful LASIK plan links your goals with corneal suitability, realistic consent and reliable aftercare. Obtain the lens instructions and screening results, compare alternatives and keep urgent contact details available before committing to treatment.

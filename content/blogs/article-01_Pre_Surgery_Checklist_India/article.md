# Complete Pre-Surgery Checklist in India: Medical Tests, Documents & Fasting Rules



Preparing for surgery involves more than reaching the hospital on time. You may need to complete medical tests, arrange documents, discuss medicines with your doctor, follow fasting instructions and plan for recovery at home.

This **pre surgery checklist India** guide explains steps patients and caregivers should understand before an elective operation.

For example, one patient may require additional blood investigations, while another may not. Similarly, fasting and medication instructions must be tailored to your circumstances.

Use this guide as a starting point, but always follow the specific instructions provided by your surgeon, anaesthesiologist and hospital.

## Why Is Pre-Surgery Preparation Important?

Pre-surgery preparation, also called *preoperative preparation*, helps your medical team assess your health and plan your treatment.

- **Safer anaesthesia planning:** Your anaesthesiologist can review your medical history, previous anaesthesia experiences and relevant investigations.
- **Appropriate risk assessment:** Your team can identify conditions that may affect surgery or recovery.
- **Better medication planning:** Some medicines need special instructions before an operation, while others should generally continue.
- **Smoother hospital admission:** Having documents and approvals ready may reduce administrative delays.
- **More informed decisions:** Understanding the procedure, alternatives and possible complications helps you participate in the consent process.

Preparation does not eliminate surgical risks, but it gives your healthcare team information to plan your care.

## Pre-Surgery Medical Tests in India

Medical investigations are part of preparation for some operations. However, **not every patient needs every test**. Your doctor selects investigations based on the planned procedure, your medical history, examination findings, existing conditions and anaesthesia requirements.

### Common preoperative tests

| Medical test | What it checks | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Complete Blood Count (CBC) | Haemoglobin, white blood cells and platelets | When anaemia, infection-related concerns or significant blood loss are relevant |
| Blood glucose / HbA1c | Current glucose levels and longer-term glucose control | Particularly for patients with diabetes or suspected abnormal glucose control |
| Kidney function tests | Creatinine, electrolytes and kidney function | For kidney disease, relevant medicines, significant comorbidities or certain procedures |
| Liver function tests | Liver enzymes and other indicators of liver health | When liver disease or a relevant clinical concern is present |
| Coagulation tests (PT/INR, aPTT) | Aspects of blood clotting | For selected bleeding disorders, anticoagulant use or other specific indications |
| ECG | Electrical activity of the heart | Depending on cardiovascular history, symptoms and surgical risk |
| Chest X-ray | Selected heart and lung findings | When symptoms, known disease or specific clinical indications justify imaging |
| Additional investigations | Procedure-specific or condition-specific concerns | When recommended by the surgical or anaesthesia team |

### Is a pre anaesthesia checkup necessary?

A pre-anaesthesia checkup, commonly called a PAC, allows the anaesthesia team to assess your suitability for the planned anaesthetic technique.

The team may examine your airway, review existing test reports and decide whether further investigations or specialist opinions are necessary.

NICE NG45 recommends selecting routine preoperative tests according to the procedure and the patient's health rather than automatically testing everyone.

## Essential Documents to Carry Before Surgery

Hospital admission involves clinical and administrative requirements.

### Personal identification and admission records

Confirm whether the hospital requires original documents, photocopies or digital copies.

### Medical records and investigations

Bring recent prescriptions, discharge summaries, relevant specialist consultations, blood test reports, ECG reports and imaging results.

### Medication and allergy information

Include over-the-counter products, supplements and herbal remedies. Record known drug allergies and the reactions you experienced.

### Health insurance and cashless admission

For insured patients in India, hospital paperwork may include:

- Insurance card or policy details
- Government or employer health scheme identification, where applicable
- Third-party administrator (TPA) information
- Pre-authorisation or cashless approval details
- Identity and other documents requested by the insurer or hospital

Cashless treatment is subject to policy terms, eligibility, network arrangements and approval. Confirm expected coverage, exclusions and any patient-payable amounts before admission.

### Consent and authorization

Some procedures may require additional consent or authorization according to applicable rules.

### How many hours should you fast before surgery?

The Indian Society of Anaesthesiologists (ISA) has published guidance on fasting and feeding around surgery. Recommendations depend on the type of intake and the patient's circumstances.

| Food or drink | Common minimum fasting interval |
|---|---|
| Clear liquids, such as plain water | 2 hours |
| Light meal, such as toast | 6 hours |
| Non-human milk or a meal containing solids | At least 6 hours, depending on composition |
| Fatty, fried food or meat | 8 hours or longer may be needed |

**These are general reference intervals, not instructions for an individual patient.** Your hospital may prescribe a different schedule based on your procedure, health conditions and anaesthesia plan.

### What counts as a clear liquid?

Thick juices, smoothies and drinks containing solid particles may also require different restrictions. Do not assume that a drink is permitted simply because it looks transparent. Follow the specific list supplied by your anaesthesia team.

### Special fasting considerations

- **Patients with diabetes:** Fasting can affect blood glucose levels, especially when combined with insulin or certain diabetes medicines. Your team should provide a plan for monitoring glucose and managing medicines.
- **Patients with delayed gastric emptying:** Conditions such as gastroparesis may increase the possibility that food remains in the stomach longer than expected.
- **Patients taking GLP-1 medicines:** Some diabetes and weight-management medicines can delay stomach emptying. Additional assessment or preparation may be necessary.
- **Emergency surgery:** Standard elective fasting arrangements may not be possible. The anaesthesia team will assess the circumstances and choose appropriate precautions.

Do not hide the information or attempt to compensate by inducing vomiting.

## Medications Before Surgery: What Should Patients Discuss?

Many patients take daily medicines for conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, heart disease or thyroid disorders.

**Never stop or change a prescribed medicine solely because you are scheduled for surgery.**

### Blood thinners

Some patients require specialist coordination, particularly after recent cardiovascular events or coronary stent placement.

### Diabetes medicines and insulin

Ask your team for specific instructions about insulin, tablets, glucose monitoring and the management of low blood sugar.

### Blood pressure and heart medicines

The correct decision depends on the medicine, its purpose and the procedure.

### GLP-1 medicines

GLP-1 receptor agonists, including semaglutide and liraglutide, may slow stomach emptying. Multisociety guidance issued in 2024 indicates that many patients can continue these medicines before elective surgery, while those at greater risk of gastrointestinal complications may need individualized precautions.

### Supplements and herbal products

Inform your healthcare team about vitamins, herbal products, nutritional supplements and over-the-counter medicines. Some products may interact with prescribed medicines or affect bleeding and anaesthesia. Your team can advise whether any changes are required.

### Questions to ask about medicines

Ask for written instructions to avoid confusion.

## Complete Pre-Surgery Checklist: 7 Days Before to Surgery Day

This practical surgery preparation checklist can help patients and caregivers organise essential tasks. The timeline is illustrative; your hospital may request certain steps earlier or later.

### 7 days before surgery

- [ ] Confirm the operation date, hospital and admission arrangements.
- [ ] Complete the pre-anaesthesia assessment as scheduled.
- [ ] Arrange only the investigations recommended by your doctor.
- [ ] Discuss existing medical conditions and previous anaesthesia problems.
- [ ] Review all prescribed medicines, supplements and allergies.
- [ ] Confirm insurance eligibility, estimated costs and approval requirements.
- [ ] Arrange transport and support for discharge, if needed.

### 3 days before surgery

- [ ] Confirm that required test results have reached your surgical team.
- [ ] Check whether additional specialist clearance is pending.
- [ ] Review written medication instructions.
- [ ] Ask about the expected hospital stay and recovery needs.
- [ ] Organise identification, medical records and insurance documents.
- [ ] Report any new fever, cough, infection or other health changes.

### 1 day before surgery

- [ ] Confirm your reporting time and hospital location.
- [ ] Follow any special bathing or skin-preparation instructions.
- [ ] Review your exact fasting start time.
- [ ] Pack comfortable clothes, toiletries and permitted personal essentials.
- [ ] Keep essential medical documents and contact details accessible.
- [ ] Confirm who will accompany you and help you return home.
- [ ] Follow your team's instructions about medicines and food.

### On the day of surgery

- [ ] Follow the prescribed fasting instructions.
- [ ] Take only the medicines specifically approved by your medical team.
- [ ] Bring your identification, records and admission documents.
- [ ] Follow hospital instructions about jewellery, contact lenses, make-up and nail products.
- [ ] Inform staff about any change in health or accidental food or drink intake.
- [ ] Confirm that your questions about the procedure and consent have been addressed.
- [ ] Arrive at the hospital at the requested time.

## Special Preparation for GI and Endocrine Surgery

Preparation can vary significantly between general surgery, gastrointestinal procedures and endocrine operations.

### Gallbladder surgery

Ask whether additional imaging is needed and how your medical conditions may affect the operation.

### Hernia surgery

Your surgeon may discuss smoking, weight management, diabetes control and activities that increase abdominal pressure.

### Thyroid surgery

Do not change thyroid medicines without medical advice.

### Gastrointestinal surgery

Operations involving the stomach or intestines may require additional nutritional assessment, imaging or other investigations.

## Common Mistakes Patients Should Avoid Before Surgery

1. **Assuming every medical test is compulsory.** Routine testing without a clinical indication can create unnecessary costs and delays.
2. **Following generic fasting advice instead of hospital instructions.** An online schedule may not apply to your condition or anaesthesia plan.
3. **Stopping medicines without medical advice.** Suddenly stopping certain medicines can create risks.
4. **Forgetting to disclose supplements or allergies.** These details can affect perioperative care.
5. **Arriving without insurance documents.** Incomplete paperwork may delay administrative processing.
6. **Ignoring new symptoms.** Report fever, respiratory symptoms, chest discomfort or a new infection promptly.
7. **Failing to plan for discharge.** After sedation or general anaesthesia, a responsible adult may need to accompany you home.
8. **Not asking questions before signing consent.** Understand the operation, significant risks, alternatives and recovery expectations.

## Questions to Ask Your Surgeon Before Surgery

1. Why is this operation recommended, and are there reasonable alternatives?
2. What are the main benefits and risks in my particular case?
3. Which preoperative tests and specialist assessments do I need?
4. What type of anaesthesia is planned?
5. Exactly when must I stop eating and drinking?
6. Which medicines should I take, withhold or adjust before surgery?
7. How long am I expected to stay in hospital?
8. What are the estimated hospital costs, and what might insurance not cover?
9. What should I expect regarding pain, wound care, diet and return to normal activities?
10. Which symptoms after discharge require urgent medical attention, and whom should I contact?

Consider writing down the answers or asking a family member to attend the consultation with you.

## Planning for Hospital Discharge and Support at Home

A pre-surgery checklist is more useful when it also considers what happens after the operation. Before admission, ask your surgeon how you will be assessed for discharge, whether a same-day return home is possible, and what assistance you may need. The answer can be very different for a minor day-care procedure and a major operation requiring inpatient monitoring. If you live alone, explain that during preoperative assessment rather than assuming you can make arrangements at the last moment.

Make sure a trusted caregiver knows the expected location of the discharge counter, how medicines will be collected, and how to contact the team if recovery does not follow the written plan. If you may need a walking aid or help with stairs, organise this before coming home. Keep emergency contact numbers in an easily accessible place and avoid booking travel immediately after an operation without asking your treating team.

### Questions to settle before discharge

- Who will explain wound care, prescribed medicines and activity restrictions to me and my caregiver?
- Which symptoms are expected, and which require a call to the surgeon or urgent hospital evaluation?
- When is the first follow-up, and does it need to be in person?
- Are there any specific instructions for bathing, diet, driving, lifting or returning to work?
- If an insurance claim is involved, which discharge records and receipts should be kept?

The ideal preparation plan combines hospital instructions with realistic support at home. Your own surgeon and anaesthesiologist remain responsible for tailoring these recommendations to your procedure and health.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What tests are required before surgery in India?

Preoperative tests depend on the type of surgery, medical history and anaesthesia assessment. Your doctor will determine which tests are appropriate.

### 2. How many hours should I fast before surgery?

Your anaesthesiologist's specific instructions always take priority.

### 3. Can I drink water before surgery?

Some patients may drink clear liquids, including water, until two hours before anaesthesia. However, this is not appropriate for everyone.

### 4. What documents are needed for surgery admission?

Requirements vary by hospital, insurer and procedure.

### 5. Can I take my regular medicines before surgery?

Some regular medicines may be continued, while others require temporary changes. Blood thinners, diabetes medicines and certain cardiovascular medicines need particular attention. Ask your doctor for a written medication plan rather than making changes yourself.

### 6. What should I carry to the hospital for surgery?

If an overnight stay is planned, ask the hospital what additional belongings are recommended.

### 7. Is an ECG compulsory before every surgery?

No. An ECG is not universally required before surgery. Its usefulness depends on cardiovascular history, existing conditions, surgical complexity and other clinical factors. Routine testing should be based on individual assessment.

### 8. What happens if I accidentally eat before surgery?

Tell the anaesthesia team immediately, including what you consumed and when. Never conceal accidental food or drink intake.

### 9. How should diabetic patients prepare for surgery?

An individualised plan is important because changes in food intake and surgical stress can affect glucose levels.

### 10. When should I contact my doctor before surgery?

Seek urgent medical care for severe symptoms such as chest pain or serious breathing difficulty.

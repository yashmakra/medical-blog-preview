# Cataract Surgery Checklist: What Every Patient Must Do Before Eye Surgery

Planning a procedure can feel complicated when appointments, medical reports and hospital paperwork arrive at different times. This **cataract surgery checklist** is designed for patients and caregivers in India who want a clear, practical explanation of what to check before admission. It covers eye tests, lens discussions, medicines, admission documents and recovery. Some steps are routine for one patient but unnecessary for another. Your own medical history and the procedure planned by your specialist determine what is actually appropriate.

Cataract surgery removes the cloudy natural lens and replaces it with a clear artificial intraocular lens. Surgery is usually considered when cloudiness interferes with vision and daily activities. The procedure is often performed as day care with local anaesthesia, although the approach differs for some patients. Before booking a date, confirm which eye will be treated and discuss what improvement you can realistically expect. Planning transport, follow-up and prescribed eye drops early can make the day less stressful.

## Understanding the Procedure and Your Treatment Options

A comprehensive eye consultation examines visual acuity, eye pressure, the cornea and retina as indicated. Biometry measures dimensions of the eye to calculate intraocular lens power. Lens choice requires discussion of near, distance and intermediate-vision expectations; no implant guarantees spectacle independence. Other eye diseases can limit how much vision improves.

## Why Preoperative Preparation Matters

Preoperative review helps the ophthalmologist and anaesthesia team understand eye disease, medicines, diabetes and other medical conditions. Tell them about earlier eye operations and anaesthesia problems. Tests and precautions are selected for your circumstances, not ordered automatically for every patient.

## Preoperative Medical Tests and Evaluations

| Test or evaluation | What it helps assess | When it may be appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| Vision and refraction | Assess visual difficulty | As part of cataract evaluation |
| Slit-lamp examination | Examine cataract and eye structures | During the ophthalmic consultation |
| Biometry | Calculate proposed lens power | Before intraocular lens implantation |
| Dilated retinal examination | Evaluate retina where possible | Based on the ophthalmologist’s examination |
| OCT or corneal testing | Assess selected macular or corneal issues | When clinically indicated |
| General medical tests | Assess important comorbidities | Not mandatory as a routine panel for every patient |

## Medicines Before Surgery: Discuss an Individual Plan

Bring a list of eye drops and all other medicines, including blood thinners and diabetes treatments. Do not stop aspirin or anticoagulants just because cataract surgery is planned. Your ophthalmologist and anaesthesia team will decide what is appropriate for the technique. Use prescribed preoperative drops exactly as instructed; not everyone receives the same drop schedule.

## Fasting and Dietary Instructions

Cataract surgery is often performed using local or topical anaesthesia, and some patients receive sedation. **Eating and drinking instructions depend on the specific anaesthesia and sedation plan**. A person having topical anaesthesia without sedation may receive different instructions from someone having deeper sedation. Do not assume that fasting from midnight is universally required, and do not eat or drink against the written instructions you receive. Ask the hospital what counts as permitted clear liquids and whether morning medicines can be taken with water.

## Documents Required for Hospital Admission in India

- Government-issued photo identification accepted by the hospital, such as Aadhaar, passport or driving licence.
- Appointment confirmation, admission letter, hospital registration number and contact information.
- Reports requested by the specialist, relevant imaging discs or digital reports, and previous discharge summaries.
- Current medicine list, written allergy information and important specialist opinions.
- Health insurance card or policy details, employer or government scheme information and cashless pre-authorisation correspondence, where applicable.
- Required consent and authorisation documents as explained by hospital staff; consent must follow a proper discussion, not merely a signature.

## Detailed Procedure-Specific Preparation

Ask which eye will be treated, what lens is planned and how the team confirms lens power. If your vision differs between eyes, discuss timing of the second operation. Tell staff about glaucoma, retinal disease, previous eye operations, difficulty lying flat, or an inability to remain still. These details affect preparation and expectations.

Cataract procedures performed with topical or local anaesthesia without sedation may have different eating and drinking instructions from operations under general anaesthesia. Never automatically apply a universal overnight fast. Clarify the actual plan in writing. Organise transport because your vision may be blurred and driving yourself home is unsafe.

## Complete Pre-Surgery Preparation Checklist

### Around 7 days before (or earlier if instructed)

- [ ] Confirm the procedure, date, hospital building and reporting arrangements.
- [ ] Attend the specialist and anaesthesia assessments as scheduled.
- [ ] Complete only tests or opinions requested by your care team.

### Around 3 days before

- [ ] Check that required reports reached the right clinician and ask about outstanding results.
- [ ] Re-read written medication instructions and flag any conflicting advice.
- [ ] Contact the hospital about cough, fever, infection, unexpected symptoms or recent treatment.

### The day before

- [ ] Verify the admission time and the exact food and drink cut-offs.
- [ ] Follow only prescribed skin, bowel or other procedure-specific preparation.
- [ ] Keep phone numbers and insurance paperwork within reach.

### On the day of the procedure

- [ ] Follow the hospital’s personal fasting and medicine plan.
- [ ] Carry ID, reports, prescriptions and admission paperwork.
- [ ] Tell staff about any new symptom, unplanned food intake or medicine taken.

## What to Bring to the Hospital

Bring identification, eye reports, medicine and allergy lists, spectacles, sunglasses, insurance details and a phone. Arrange an escort home; do not drive yourself.

## What to Expect After Surgery

Vision may be blurry at first and eye drops are often prescribed for healing or infection prevention. Avoid rubbing the eye and use a shield if instructed. Worsening pain, sudden vision loss, increasing redness or flashes and floaters merit urgent ophthalmic advice. Follow the personalised schedule for water exposure, activity and follow-up.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Ordering every test advertised online:** Investigations should answer clinical questions and match the treating team's plan.
- **Copying another patient’s fasting schedule:** Your anaesthesia, comorbidities and timing can make the correct instructions different.
- **Stopping medicines independently:** A decision about blood thinners or diabetes treatment may require coordination among specialists.
- **Forgetting to mention symptoms:** Recent fever, infection, chest symptoms or medication changes can affect medical planning.
- **Leaving insurance checks until admission:** A policy card does not guarantee a particular cashless approval or benefit.
- **Planning only for the day of surgery:** Transport, follow-up, wound care and home support are also part of preparation.
- **Expecting an intraocular lens to remove every vision problem:** Other eye conditions and lens choices influence the visual outcome.

## Questions to Ask Your Specialist

1. What diagnosis or findings make this procedure suitable for me?
2. What non-surgical or alternative treatments should I consider?
3. What are the most important risks in my individual case?
4. Which tests and medical clearances actually need completing?
5. What kind of anaesthesia, fasting and medication plan will I follow?
6. Which intraocular lens is planned and why?
7. Could glaucoma or retinal disease affect the result?
8. Will sedation be used, and do I need to fast?
9. Which eye drops start before surgery?
10. How soon should I seek help for vision changes?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Do I need to fast for cataract surgery?

It depends on the planned anaesthesia and sedation; follow your eye team’s instructions.

### 2. Are all blood tests required?

No. Routine general tests depend on health conditions and anaesthesia plans.

### 3. Can I drive myself home?

No; arrange safe transport because vision and alertness may be affected.

### 4. Will I need glasses afterwards?

Possibly. The outcome depends on lens selection, healing and other eye conditions.

### 5. What symptoms are urgent after cataract surgery?

Severe pain, sudden decreased vision and marked increasing redness require prompt eye assessment.

### 6. Which tests are required for cataract surgery checklist?

Tests are selected after examining your medical history, procedure and anaesthesia needs. Not everyone needs an identical panel.

### 7. Can I take my usual medicines on the morning of surgery?

Only according to your written plan. Different treatments require different instructions, and you should not independently stop prescribed medicines.

### 8. Should I continue glaucoma eye drops before cataract surgery?

Tell your ophthalmologist exactly which drops you use. Continue, pause or adjust them only according to the specialist’s written instructions.

## Conclusion

Use this **cataract surgery checklist** to prepare questions, documents and transport. Follow your ophthalmologist’s instructions about eye drops, anaesthesia and review. Sudden loss of vision or severe pain needs urgent eye care.

# Robotic Surgery Preparation Checklist: What Patients Should Know Beforehand

When a surgeon recommends a robotic approach, ask first which operation you need and why. Robot-assisted surgery describes how instruments are controlled; it does not identify the condition being treated or replace the surgeon's judgement. Preparing for a robotic prostate operation, bowel resection or gynaecological procedure can involve very different tests, risks and recovery arrangements.

This robotic surgery checklist helps you separate the device discussion from the clinical preparation your actual operation requires. It covers questions about the surgeon and team, alternatives, possible conversion to another approach, anaesthesia, medicines and the costs that may appear in an Indian hospital estimate. It also explains what details should be written down before admission.

Technology should support an appropriate treatment plan, rather than become the reason for choosing treatment. Ask how the proposed approach changes the decision for your diagnosis and what evidence supports the expected benefit. A small skin incision does not make an operation minor. Use the guide alongside the procedure-specific instructions from your surgeon and anaesthesiologist, especially for fasting, bowel preparation and changes to regular medicines.

## What the robot does, and what it does not do

In commonly used robot-assisted surgical systems, the surgeon operates controls that move instruments through a bedside platform. A camera provides a view of the operative area, and other trained staff support the procedure at the patient side. The system is not an independent doctor deciding what tissue to remove.

The [FDA's patient recommendations on computer-assisted surgical systems](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/surgery-devices/computer-assisted-surgical-systems) advise discussing risks, benefits and the surgeon's training and experience. They also emphasise that the approach is not appropriate in every situation. Device regulation in the United States does not establish the approval or suitability of a particular use in India; ask your hospital about the actual system and intended procedure.

### Compare approaches for the same operation

Ask how robotic surgery compares with conventional laparoscopy, open surgery and any non-surgical options for your diagnosis. The comparison should consider important outcomes, not only incision size or hospital advertising. Discuss the surgeon's experience with each option and what happens if the preferred approach cannot be completed safely.

For cancer, ask about the intended cancer operation and whether the proposed approach has suitable evidence for that particular disease. A claim about better visualisation or instrument movement does not establish better long-term cancer control. Do not treat a hospital's ownership of a robot as proof of superior outcomes.

## Make a procedure-specific preparation map

Write the exact operation, organ and side if relevant on your planning sheet. Confirm whether additional procedures, implant placement or lymph-node surgery may be part of the consent discussion. Ask whether final decisions depend on imaging, pathology or findings during the operation.

| Planned operation type | Preparation discussion | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Urological surgery | Urine testing when indicated, urinary symptoms and possible catheter care | Preparation depends on the urinary procedure and infection risk |
| Colorectal surgery | Nutrition, bowel preparation only if prescribed, and possible stoma counselling | The operative plan may affect bowel recovery and discharge support |
| Gynaecological surgery | Imaging, bleeding history, fertility wishes and possible change in extent | These concerns come from the condition, not the robotic platform |
| Other abdominal or thoracic surgery | Organ-specific staging, functional assessment and recovery needs | Different operations require different clinical pathways |

This table is a conversation guide, not a list of mandatory tests. Ask for the checklist for your exact procedure. If two departments issue conflicting instructions, request a coordinated written plan rather than deciding which one to follow yourself.

## Medical evaluation before a robotic operation

The preoperative assessment should review your diagnosis, activity level, previous operations and health conditions. Explain heart or lung disease, sleep apnoea, diabetes, mobility limitations and previous anaesthesia problems. Prior abdominal surgery or scarring may affect planning, but its significance must be assessed by the surgeon.

Bring full imaging series, pathology reports and earlier operative notes where available. Some questions cannot be answered from a report summary alone. Ask what further investigations will change the plan and whether suitable recent tests can be accepted. [NICE NG45](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng45/chapter/recommendations) provides a framework for selected preoperative testing rather than blanket test packages.

| Investigation or assessment | Purpose | When it may be recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Procedure-specific imaging or pathology review | Confirms anatomy and treatment requirements | According to the disease and planned operation |
| Blood count | Assesses anaemia or other blood concerns | Bleeding history, illness or operative risk warrants it |
| Kidney function and glucose-related tests | Informs medicines and perioperative management | Comorbidity, treatment or operation makes them relevant |
| ECG or cardiorespiratory evaluation | Investigates fitness concerns | Symptoms and individual risk justify assessment |
| Urine or other infection testing | Looks for infection relevant to the procedure | When indicated by the organ and clinical history |

## Positioning and anaesthesia deserve attention

Many robotic operations require general anaesthesia. Some abdominal or pelvic procedures use gas to create working space and a tilted operating position. Explain restricted neck or joint movement, back pain, eye concerns and breathing difficulties so the anaesthesia team can assess relevant risks and positioning needs.

Ask how the team protects pressure points and nerves, manages breathing during the procedure and plans pain control afterwards. The appropriate discussion depends on the operation and expected duration. A promise of an almost painless experience should not replace an explanation of pain medicines, nausea treatment and postoperative observation.

Tell the anaesthesiologist about previous airway difficulty, loose teeth, reflux, sleep apnoea or regular CPAP use. Ask which equipment to bring. If you develop a new chest infection or another significant illness before admission, report it; the team decides whether the plan remains appropriate.

## Medicines, fasting and bowel instructions

List every prescription, injection, painkiller, supplement and herbal product. Record why blood thinners are prescribed and when weekly medicines were last taken. Diabetes medicines, including weight-management injections where applicable, require a plan suited to your clinical and anaesthesia assessment. Do not use a universal stopping interval found online.

Follow the hospital's written fasting instructions, including permitted drinks and approved morning medicines. Reduced eating in the days before surgery may also require a glucose-monitoring plan. Explain accidental food or drink intake, vomiting or problems taking medicines rather than concealing them to avoid a delay.

Bowel cleansing is relevant to some operations, not to robotic surgery as a category. Similarly, special diets, hair removal, antiseptic washing and antibiotics depend on the actual procedure. Ask the team to specify the product, timing and reason if something is prescribed. Do not add extra preparation because the operation uses advanced equipment.

## Discuss conversion and consent before the day

An operation may need to change to standard laparoscopy or open surgery if anatomy, bleeding, equipment problems or another safety concern requires it. Ask how the team prepares for that possibility and how it might affect recovery and charges. Conversion is a clinical contingency, not necessarily evidence that someone made an error.

Confirm who will operate, who covers if the named surgeon becomes unavailable and what experience the wider theatre team has with the procedure. Reasonable questions include emergency arrangements, access to blood products and critical-care support when needed. There is no single number of previous robotic cases that independently proves competence for every operation.

## Understand the estimate and follow-up arrangements

In India, a quoted robotic package may include a separate technology or instrument charge. Ask whether the estimate includes surgeon and anaesthesia fees, reusable or disposable instruments, implants, pathology, room category, medicines and expected follow-up. Clarify charges if conversion, extra procedures or a longer stay becomes necessary.

Check cashless authorisation for the exact procedure and technology component. Approval for a hospital admission may not cover every charge. Avoid paying a non-refundable technology premium before understanding the clinical alternatives and the hospital's terms.

If you travel for surgery, decide where wound, catheter, drain or rehabilitation reviews will happen. Ask which problems can be discussed remotely and which need examination. Have a responsible escort, realistic accommodation plans and a way to obtain the operative summary and pathology results.

## Robotic surgery preparation checklist

### Before choosing the approach

- [ ] Record the exact operation and reason for treatment.
- [ ] Compare robotic, laparoscopic, open and other relevant alternatives.
- [ ] Ask about the surgeon's procedure-specific training and experience.
- [ ] Understand conversion, possible additional procedures and consent limits.
- [ ] Request an itemised estimate and insurance decision.

### Before admission

- [ ] Complete the organ-specific assessment recommended for you.
- [ ] Obtain a coordinated medicine, diet and fasting plan.
- [ ] Follow bowel preparation only when specifically prescribed.
- [ ] Report new illness or changes in health.
- [ ] Carry ID, imaging, pathology, medicine details and authorisation documents.

### Before discharge

- [ ] Know how to manage any catheter, drain, dressing or stoma.
- [ ] Understand movement, work and travel restrictions for the actual operation.
- [ ] Confirm review appointments and the urgent contact route.

## Recovery and warning signs depend on the organ

Small entry wounds do not reveal the extent of internal surgery. Ask about pain, fluid intake, bowel or urinary function and early movement. The team should explain what needs to happen before discharge and which symptoms are expected for your operation.

Seek urgent assessment for significant bleeding, severe or worsening pain, persistent vomiting, inability to drink, fever with illness or a problem with a necessary catheter or drain. Chest pain, breathlessness, collapse or sudden neurological symptoms require emergency care. Add the specific warning signs for your organ procedure to your discharge plan.

## Six mistakes to avoid

1. **Choosing a device before choosing an appropriate treatment.** Start with diagnosis and alternatives.
2. **Assuming the robot works autonomously.** Understand the surgeon's and bedside team's roles.
3. **Using a robotic label as a quality ranking.** Ask for procedure-specific evidence and experience.
4. **Taking bowel preparation for every robotic operation.** It is not universally required.
5. **Ignoring the possibility of conversion.** Discuss the contingency and financial implications beforehand.
6. **Expecting instant recovery from small incisions.** Internal healing and restrictions still matter.

## Questions for the surgical team

1. Why is the robotic approach appropriate for this diagnosis?
2. What meaningful benefits are expected compared with the alternatives?
3. Which outcomes are uncertain or similar between approaches?
4. Who operates the console and who supports the patient at the bedside?
5. What circumstances could require conversion?
6. What positioning or anaesthesia concerns are relevant to me?
7. Which preparation belongs to my organ operation rather than the device?
8. Which additional technology charges might insurance exclude?
9. Who manages problems and follow-up after discharge?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a robotic operation always minimally invasive?

The term often describes an instrument-control platform used through small incisions, but the exact approach varies. Ask what is planned and whether an open incision may be required.

### Does the robot replace the surgeon?

No. In commonly used systems the surgeon controls the instruments, supported by a trained theatre team. Ask about the actual system rather than assumptions from the name.

### Will the preparation tests be different because a robot is used?

Most testing is driven by the disease, operation, anaesthesia and your health. Technology-specific considerations may be added, but a robot does not create one universal test panel.

### Do I need bowel cleansing?

Only if your procedure team prescribes it. An abdominal or pelvic robotic operation does not automatically require a full bowel cleanse.

### Is conversion to open surgery a complication?

It can be a planned safety response to a difficult situation. Ask why it may be needed and how the team would explain its implications for your recovery.

### Does a robotic approach guarantee better cancer outcomes?

No. Discuss evidence for the exact cancer operation, including long-term outcomes where available. Technical features alone cannot establish a survival benefit.

### Can I compare two robotic packages by price?

Only after checking that the operation, team, instruments, room category and follow-up components match. A lower advertised figure may exclude items included elsewhere.

### When can I return to normal activity?

Follow the restrictions for your actual internal operation, not the size of the wounds. Ask about lifting, driving, work, sexual activity and travel before discharge.

## Keep technology in its clinical context

A useful robotic surgery plan explains why the approach fits your diagnosis, who will deliver it and what happens if circumstances change. Pair that discussion with the correct organ-specific preparation, clear costs and a dependable recovery pathway.
